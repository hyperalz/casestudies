01 · Ocado Technology · Retail technology

Moving a fast-growing tech business from projects to products

Ocado was about to sell its technology to grocers abroad. I was its first product hire, brought in to help it organise around products rather than projects — and to make sure the first international client wasn’t the one to find the platform’s weak spots.

My role
First product hire: Product Owner and Product Operations lead, later Acting Head of Product
Scale
600+ engineers, 22 Product Owners, three legacy systems being rebuilt as one platform
Worked with
General Manager, Heads of Engineering and Operations, Product Owners

The situation

When I joined in 2013, Ocado Technology ran its work as projects. It had to keep three legacy systems running while rebuilding them into what became the Ocado Smart Platform (OSP), the modular system that now runs online grocery operations for retailers around the world.

The commercial stakes were high. The first international partner was about to depend on software that hadn’t yet been proven in live operation, an exposure estimated at £50m. Most Product Owners were new to the role, and many people were comfortable with traditional project management and wary of changing it.

What I did

  • Built the product craft. I coached more than 20 Product Owners through a community of practice, workshops and one-to-ones, and mentored heads of department and team leads on product thinking.
  • Created a safe place to fail. I set up an internal “start-up”: a simulated retailer running on OSP, so we could use the platform in real conditions and learn from it before a paying client relied on it.
  • Connected strategy to teams. I built a portfolio-level operational roadmap that fed directly into team Kanban boards, so a retailer’s need could be traced from request to release across many teams.
  • Kept both worlds running. I managed planning, scope, budget, risks and dependencies for the replatforming while the legacy estate kept serving customers.
  • Changed how teams worked. I introduced Scrum and Kanban with an emphasis on transparency and continuous discovery, working through resistance one team at a time.

What changed

£50mfirst-client risk mitigated by validating OSP in-house first
20+Product Owners coached into a shared product practice
1 platformOSP launched and later scaled to partners worldwide

OSP launched without passing unproven components to its first client. The product-centric model we put in place supported Ocado’s shift from grocer to platform business, and product decisions got faster because teams were learning from real use rather than from specifications.

What I took from it

You can’t announce a product operating model. People need a safe place to practise it. The internal start-up changed more minds than any training session did.

02 · Investec · Corporate & Investment Banking

Building an FX and hedging platform inside a regulated bank

Corporate FX and hedging at Investec ran on spreadsheets, manual structuring and siloed systems. As part of the senior leadership team of a new unit with its own P&L, I led product and delivery to replace that with a modern, client-facing platform, without increasing operational risk.

My role
Consultant, Product-Led Transformation and Delivery; part of the senior leadership team
Scale
Greenfield team with its own P&L, grown from about 7 to 30 people
Worked with
Product, Engineering and Commercial leadership; corporate clients

The situation

Demand for faster, clearer currency risk management was growing, but the operation behind it was slow and manual. Building something new meant doing it inside a bank: strict compliance, a hierarchical culture, and teams that were used to working in silos and to prototyping more than shipping.

What I did

  • Aligned the leadership. I worked with Product, Engineering and Commercial leaders to agree strategy, funding and delivery priorities, so teams weren’t pulled in competing directions.
  • Built the team. I led hiring from about 7 to 30, set up cross-functional product teams, and coached them in Scrum, Kanban and BDD.
  • Replatformed fast and safely. In about four months we moved the estate onto the new platform, put low-value trades online and turned spreadsheet-based structuring into software, while clients kept trading.
  • Built with clients, not just for them. We designed the Hedging Analytics tool alongside corporate clients, giving them round-the-clock oversight of FX positions and automated, dynamic hedging.
  • Reduced dependency friction. I pushed an open-API approach and continuous delivery so teams could release independently and decisions didn’t queue behind one another.

What changed

~4 monthsto replatform the legacy estate
7 → 30team grown while keeping delivery momentum
12 weeksto ship a business savings account with a team of 12

Clients got clearer insight into their currency exposure and more control over their hedging. The capability we built contributed to Investec being named Best Business FX Provider in the Moneyfacts Awards in 2024 and 2025.

What I took from it

In a regulated business, speed comes from making risk visible early, not from cutting corners. Once compliance could see what we were doing, it stopped being a brake.

03 · Caerphilly County Borough Council · Public sector

Running a portfolio of cloud migrations for a public body

Several of the council’s suppliers were retiring their on-premise products at once. The people who had to move were busy running services and had never done a migration before. Through my consultancy, Foam Works Digital, I led a run of migrations that became a repeatable way of working.

My role
Director, Foam Works Digital — outcomes-based delivery partner to the council
Scope
HR, Occupational Health and schools systems, plus facilitation for the Housing team
Worked with
Digital Leadership Group, service teams, 85 schools and four suppliers

The situation

The council’s Customer Digital Strategy committed it to the cloud. In practice, each migration came with the same problems: little in-house migration experience, staff borrowed from their day jobs, uneasy supplier relationships and fixed contract windows. Each one also touched something sensitive — payroll, clinical records, children’s data.

What I did

HR platform (iTrent)

Supplier accountability and cost control

I set clear roles and escalation routes with the supplier, took over day-to-day budget control to stop consultancy days overrunning, and gave leadership a simple one-page view of whether we were on track.

Delivered on time and within budget, and led to further work with the council.

Occupational Health (Cority)

Unblocking a stalled migration

I negotiated supplier terms for a four-month migration, something the council hadn’t managed to agree in four years. We tested data and configuration with clinicians and end users, and safeguarding and data protection were built in from the start.

Migrated in four months, with no disruption to employee health clinics.

Schools (SIMS, 85 schools)

A repeatable process, built with users

No school had done this before, so we designed the process with early adopters and moved schools in batches of five or six, improving the approach after each batch. Communication was frequent, open and two-way.

All 85 schools migrated successfully.

Housing (Civica Cx)

One-day facilitated reset, October 2024

A troubled system rollout was hurting morale. I ran a full-day workshop that reframed the team’s purpose and measures around residents (“fewer people homeless today”) and tested the hypothesis that the system could lift case handling from about 2,000 to 3,000 a year.

The team left with a working group, supplier evidence and clear next steps.

“Initial planning was great, we felt very supported.” “It all just worked!”Feedback from schools after their migration

What changed

Migrations stopped being one-off ordeals. The council now has a repeatable approach for future digital work: small batches, early adopters, firm supplier accountability and plain reporting to leadership.

What I took from it

In the public sector the technology is rarely the hard part. The work is holding suppliers to account and making it safe for people with no migration experience to learn as they go.

04 · Shoogle · Founder

Founding a workplace wellbeing app on a start-up budget

Employers mostly learn how their people are doing through slow annual surveys, and hybrid working hides problems like isolation until they turn into absence. I founded Shoogle to give teams a light-touch way to stay connected and give leaders an early signal. Agencies quoted around £1m to build it, so we found another way.

My role
Founder: research, product strategy and delivery, end to end
Scale
Five-person near-shore development team, iOS and Android
Worked with
Employees, HR and benefits leaders, healthcare providers

What I did

  • Started with research. I spent four months on qualitative research into what employees keep hidden and what actually motivates them. The answer was connection more than tracking, and it changed the product.
  • Scoped hard. The MVP focused on communities, private buddies and journaling, peer recognition and one-second “pulse” check-ins, with real-time dashboards for employers.
  • Ran lean delivery. I hired and led a near-shore team of five and covered every role except development myself: strategy, scope, budget and risk.
  • Used AI early. We used language models, including GPT-3, alongside classical classifiers to find themes and sentiment in pulse data and flag hotspots for early support.
  • Tested the market. I pitched to HR, benefits and healthcare buyers through demos, podcasts and trade shows.

What changed

£35kbuild cost, against £1m vendor quotes
2platforms: launch-ready on iOS and Android
4 monthsof user research shaping the MVP

Shoogle reached a launch-ready product and was validated with early users and buyers, for a fraction of what the market said it would cost.

What I took from it

Spending my own money changed how I think about everyone else’s. I now push teams hard on the smallest thing that would prove them right or wrong.

How I work

Clarity before speed

A small number of agreed priorities, and a clear sense of who decides what, does more for flow than any framework.

Just enough structure

Governance should let teams act on their own while keeping the organisation aligned. It should never become the work itself.

Make risk visible early

Test in real conditions, with real users, before the stakes are real. That’s how a £50m exposure becomes a manageable one.

A pace people can sustain

High-performing teams are built on trust and psychological safety. Teams that burn out don’t deliver consistently.
